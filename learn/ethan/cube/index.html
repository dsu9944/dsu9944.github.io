Connecting Ethan's GAN 356 i3

The 356 i3 is a GAN Gen2 smart cube. Chrome can talk to it over Bluetooth (this site is HTTPS). The cube encrypts its moves with a key based on its MAC address, so Chrome sometimes has to ask for that address once.

  1. Use Google Chrome on a computer or Android phone (not an iPhone or iPad).
  2. Wake the cube by turning a face. It should have some charge.
  3. Tap Connect cube and pick the device whose name starts with GAN (often something like GAN356i3).
  4. We'll try to read the MAC address automatically from the cube's Bluetooth advertisement. This often works on Windows, Linux and Android. It usually does not work on a Mac, because Chrome hides the real address there.
  5. If a box asks for the MAC (six pairs like AB:12:CD:34:EF:56), enter it once. We save it in this browser and shouldn't ask again.
    • Windows, Linux or Android: in Chrome open chrome://bluetooth-internals/#devices, scan, wake the cube, and copy the Address of the GAN device. (On a Mac this address is fake — don't use it.)
    • On a Mac, or if that page doesn't help: open the official GAN Cube Station app, connect the i3, and look for the Bluetooth / MAC address in the cube info. It may also be on the box or a sticker.
  6. After it connects you should see the battery and a live cube that follows Ethan's turns — even if the cube wasn't solved when you connected. If the picture doesn't match the real cube, tap Cube looks wrong? Re-sync. If the real cube is actually solved, tap My cube is solved.

Optional: turning on chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features and restarting Chrome can help auto-detect the MAC, but it isn't required once the address is saved.
